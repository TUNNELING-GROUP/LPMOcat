About LPMOcat

LPMOcat is a catalogue for exploring the structure–function relationships of lytic polysaccharide monooxygenases (LPMOs). It brings together the structures, annotations and per-residue properties of characterised LPMOs in one structural alignment, to help compare them and to support enzyme engineering.

About LPMOs

Lytic polysaccharide monooxygenases are copper-dependent enzymes that oxidatively cleave polysaccharides such as cellulose and chitin, boosting the degradation of biomass. They form the auxiliary activity families AA9–AA11 and AA13–AA17 of the CAZy database.

Their activity relies on a conserved histidine brace that coordinates the copper ion. Catalytic efficiency and substrate specificity are shaped by the architecture of the substrate-binding surface, the copper second-sphere residues and the residues that can carry electrons along hole-hopping pathways.

What each protein comes with

  • 3D structure: the mature chain from the N-terminal histidine, superposed on all others
  • Structural elements: domains, secondary structure, core strands β1–β9, loops L2, L3, LS, L8 and LC, the histidine brace and second-sphere residues, and the residues of the substrate-binding surface
  • Annotations: taxonomy, EC numbers, substrates and regioselectivity with their papers, UniProt entries and publications
  • Properties: solvent accessibility, charges, hydrophobicity, flexibility and electron-transfer paths
